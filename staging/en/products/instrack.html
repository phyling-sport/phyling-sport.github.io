INSTRACK

INSTRACK

Introduction

INSTRACK (instrumented track) is a modular running track fitted with force sensors that measures the vertical ground reaction force (vGRF) at every step of the runner. Unlike a conventional force plate, its length can be adjusted: simply add sensors and plates to measure several consecutive strides, directly in the field (athletics track, sports hall, laboratory). These measurements make it possible to build the runner's biomechanical profile: contact time, cadence, step length, impact peak, loading rate, impulse, right/left asymmetry, etc.

Each sensor can support 2 to 6 triangle vertices. This way, even if the foot lands across two triangles, the force is still measured: the total force is obtained by summing the forces of all sensors. The number of sensors and their layout can be adapted to the intended application (running, walking, other tasks).

Sensor and plate

Each triangular plate rests on force sensors by its three tips. The tips fit into machined studs screwed onto the sensors.

Access ramps

As the track is raised (≈ 12.5 cm), ramps can be placed at each end so that the runner reaches track height without changing their stride. The ramp length depends on the desired slope:

User guide

I. Setting up the track

  1. Choose a flat, clear area: the track must be laid on flat ground (athletics track, sports hall floor…) to ensure measurement quality.
  2. Assemble the aluminium rails and check that the sensors are firmly attached.
  3. Place the triangular plates on the sensors, fitting each tip into its stud.
  4. If needed, install the access ramps at each end.
  5. Connect the sensors to the acquisition system and make sure no cable runs through the running area.

II. Calibration

INSTRACK has two calibration procedures: one for the offsets (no-load values of the sensors) and one for the calibration coefficients (conversion of the signal into force). These two procedures are separate.

Offset calibration

Before each session, the sensors are zeroed: the track must be empty (nobody on it) for a few seconds. This value is sensitive to temperature and to reassembling the track; redo it after each installation.

Coefficient calibration

Each sensor is calibrated on a calibration bench: static forces from 0 to 1500 N are applied to both the sensor and a reference sensor. A linear regression gives the coefficient specific to each sensor. We recommend repeating this calibration regularly; Phyling supports this procedure.

III. Recording data

  1. Switch on the acquisition system and check that all sensors are connected.
  2. Start the recording from the Phyling application, in the Hub's Realtime tab.
  3. Have the athlete run along the full length of the track at a constant speed. Repeat the runs (for example 20 to 30 runs per speed) to obtain enough steps for each foot.
  4. Stop the recording. The data are automatically sent to the Hub.

Tip

Starting a few metres before the track allows the runner to reach a steady speed by the time they arrive on the instrumented area.

IV. Data processing

  1. Associate: In the Data tab, find the recordings associated with an athlete. Unassociated data from a recent recording can be accessed by clicking the NEW button (in blue). Click on a recording to associate it.
  2. View: Click on an associated recording to view the data, make selections and export the data in csv format.

Data

The forces measured by each sensor are zeroed and then summed to obtain the total vertical force applied to the track. Each step is then detected and assigned to the right or left foot, which makes it possible to compute many running technique indicators. The validity of the track was demonstrated in the scientific article A size-adjustable instrumented track to measure vertical ground reaction forces: a validation study (IEEE Sensors Journal, 2025): excellent agreement with a reference force plate (ICC > 0.98).

Centre of pressure (CoP) measurement

In addition to the vertical force, INSTRACK computes the centre of pressure (CoP): the point on the ground where the force is applied at each instant of the step. Tracking its movement shows how the foot rolls over the ground, from the heel to the forefoot. The CoP is also used as the point of application of the force to estimate joint loads (inverse dynamics), directly in the field.

Calculation

The position of each sensor on the track is known. The CoP is the average of the sensor positions, weighted by the force measured by each one: the more force a sensor receives, the closer the CoP is to it.

Accuracy: the study

The accuracy of the INSTRACK CoP was assessed against two reference force plates (AMTI), which compute the CoP from the moments they measure.

Protocol

  • 13 experienced runners (9 men, 4 women; 22.8 ± 3.4 years).
  • 30 runs at each of 2 self-selected speeds (jogging and fast), i.e. 60 trials per runner.
  • On a 30 m loop: runs over the 2 reference force plates on the way out, over INSTRACK (6.3 × 0.51 m) on the way back.
  • Acquisition at 1000 Hz; 2nd-order low-pass Butterworth filter at 30 Hz; contact detected above 50 N; 30 steps kept per runner, foot and speed.

Difference = RMSD (root mean square difference) between the mean CoP of the reference force plate and of INSTRACK.

  • The mean difference is below 8 mm along the running direction and below 4 mm across the width, whatever the pace. For comparison, the literature reports 4 to 6.5 mm of CoP error on instrumented treadmills.
  • The CoP path, from the heel to the forefoot, is the same with both devices.
  • The vertical force was checked beforehand: 2 to 3% difference, lower than the step-to-step variability.

During the step, accuracy depends on the force level: it is lower at the very beginning (foot strike) and the very end (toe-off), when the force is still low.

Key takeaway

INSTRACK provides a usable point of application of the force in running (≈ 7 mm difference along the running direction, ≈ 3.5 mm across the width), with reduced accuracy at foot strike and toe-off, when the force is low.

[!NOTE] The measurements on the reference force plate and on INSTRACK were not simultaneous (way out and way back): part of the difference may come from stride changes between runs rather than from INSTRACK. More details on the Publications — Running page.

Biomechanical report

From the recordings, Phyling generates a running biomechanical report for each tested speed. It brings together the athlete profile, the right/left asymmetry, the force curves of each step (right foot in blue, left foot in orange; the thick line is the mean of all measured steps) and the table of mean values per foot.

[!NOTE] Running technique is not a universal model: it is the best compromise the body spontaneously finds between all these parameters. They vary with speed, type of shoe, sex, age, weight… and fatigue.

The report appendix defines each term, explains the effect of increasing or decreasing each parameter and gives comparison values (men and women, from 10 to 16 km/h).
